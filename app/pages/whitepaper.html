Executive Summary

A guiding overview of the MyMantra system and how to utilize its outputs.

What is MyMantra?

MyMantra is a quantitative stock screener built exclusively for the Indonesian Stock Exchange (IDX). It is designed to identify the probability of institutional accumulation by analyzing market microstructure data specific to Indonesia—namely, transparent broker transaction flow, free-float pressure, and liquidity.

Unlike standard technical screeners, MyMantra looks under the hood of price action. In Indonesia, every equity transaction is publicly attributed to a broker code. This unique market feature allows us to observe whether institutional entities are accumulating a stock, and critically, whether retail participants are exiting.

How to Use the Output

The system processes all ~870 listed Indonesian equities and categorizes them into actionable, priority-ordered decision labels. You should use these labels to guide your fundamental and technical research, not as blind trading signals.

🟢 INVEST

Score ≥ 55 AND Breakout Signal Active. Institutional accumulation is clear, and the price is confirming the move with above-average volume. Prime candidates for immediate analysis.

🟡 WATCH_EXEC

Score ≥ 55 but NO breakout. Strong underlying accumulation is occurring quietly. Add to priority watchlist and wait for price confirmation before entry.

🟡 WATCH

Score 45–54. Accumulation is building. Monitor over the coming days to see if the score increases.

🟠 OBSERVE

Score 35–44. On the radar, but no actionable or sustained signal yet.

🔴 AVOID

Score < 35, or suspended by the OJK. Unfavorable flow dynamics or regulatory risk.

⚫ ILLIQUID

Average daily traded value < IDR 500M. Cannot be traded in meaningful size. Excluded from all metrics.

Key Takeaway for Users

Look for stocks transitioning from WATCH_EXEC to INVEST. Pay special attention to stocks where retail brokers (like XL/XC/YP) are net sellers while institutions are absorbing the volume. Always verify liquidity before execution.

1. The Indonesian Context

Market Microstructure and the Value of Broker Flow.

In quantitative finance, tracking institutional accumulation is notoriously difficult. In most developed markets, order-flow data is proprietary, aggregated, or anonymized. The Indonesian Stock Exchange (IDX) presents a unique structural advantage.

In Indonesia, equity transactions are publicly attributed to specific broker codes at the end of the trading day. This transparency provides an empirical window into the behavior of different market participants. It is possible to observe, in near real-time, whether institutional brokers are accumulating or distributing a stock.

Structurally Important Signals on the IDX

  • ■
    Institutional Net Buying: When large institutional and market-maker brokerages buy significantly above their own historical averages, it strongly suggests informed accumulation.
  • ■
    Retail Net Selling: Retail participation in Indonesia is heavily concentrated in a few platforms (Stockbit/XL, Ajaib/XC, Mirae/YP). When these specific retail brokers are net sellers, supply overhang is removed, allowing institutions to absorb shares quietly without spiking the price immediately.
  • ■
    Absorption Ratio: If institutional buying outpaces retail selling, the free float tightens. Consequently, the price impact of any subsequent buying pressure increases exponentially.

MyMantra systematically captures these relationships. It strictly avoids pseudoscientific trading concepts, focusing instead on empirical volume distribution, standard deviations of broker activity, and the quantitative mechanics of supply and demand within the isolated Indonesian ecosystem.

Stage 1: Proxy Scoring Components

The first stage computes five feature scores (0–100) based on aggregated data. The classes used are Institutional, Market Maker, Emitent (issuer-affiliated), Crossing (arbitrage), and Retail.

1. Broker Flow (40%)

Stage 1 uses a proxy from aggregate broker classes; Stage 2 replaces it with real Index Alpha data including z-score sustained buyers and absorption ratio.

2. Float Pressure (30%)

Measures turnover relative to the free float. Identifies how rapidly the available supply is changing hands.

3. Liquidity (20%)

Filters un-tradable stocks. Must average > IDR 500M/day. Evaluates the spread proxy (high-low range vs close).

4. Structure (10%)

Price/volume structure relative to the IHSG index (e.g., 20-day range compression, MA alignment).

Note on Catalyst (0% weight): Corporate events (dividends, splits, rights issues) within specific windows are logged for informational context but do not mathematically alter the investment score, preserving the purity of the flow signal.

3. Stage 2 Scoring & Amplification

Z-Scores, Retail Trends, and the Wurgler-Zhuravskaya Float Amplifier.

For the top 100 tickers selected from Stage 1, MyMantra fetches per-broker transaction volumes going back 60 trading days via the Index Alpha API.

Z-Score Sustained Buyers

For every active non-retail broker, the system computes a Z-score against that broker's own historical baseline over the last 20+ days:

z = (net_volume_today - μ_historical) / σ_historical

A broker is flagged as "buying significantly above average" when z ≥ 1.5. Brokers crossing this threshold add a "Presence Boost" to the ticker's score (Market Makers +12, Emitent +10, Institutions +8, Crossing +6; capped at +30 total).

The Float Amplifier (Interaction Term)

Inspired by the academic framework of Wurgler & Zhuravskaya (2002), which demonstrates that stocks with fewer arbitrage substitutes (tight free floats) experience larger price reactions to demand shocks, MyMantra applies a non-linear Float Amplifier.

The intuition: An institution buying 10,000 lots of a low-float stock moves the price significantly more than buying 10,000 lots of a highly liquid, large-cap stock.

Figure 1: Float Amplifier Multiplier Curve based on Free Float Percentage.

float_amplifier = clip( log(1 / free_float_pct) / log(20), 0, 1 )
interaction_bonus = 7.0 × float_amplifier × (broker_flow_real_score / 100)

Retail Trend Boost

Because retail platforms XL (Stockbit), XC (Ajaib) and YP (Mirae HOTS) represent the bulk of retail participation in Indonesia, their sustained net selling is an empirical indicator of retail exhaustion. If these platforms are net sellers for 3 or more of the last 10 trading days, a Trend Boost (up to +5 points) is applied, heavily biased toward low-float equities.

4. Anomaly Detection via Isolation Forest

A multi-dimensional approach to unusual broker activity.

While the Z-score is fast and interpretable, it assumes broker net volume is approximately normally distributed. To complement this, MyMantra implements an Isolation Forest (Liu, Ting, & Zhou, 2008) machine learning model trained per-broker.

The Isolation Forest makes no distributional assumptions. It identifies observations that are "easy to isolate" from the rest of the historical data—meaning they sit in sparse regions of the multidimensional feature space.

Feature Space (Per Broker, Per Day)

net_volume: Raw net lots (buy - sell)
buy_volume: Raw buy lots
sell_volume: Raw sell lots
buy_ratio: buy / (buy + sell)
net_ratio: net / (buy + sell)

The model is strictly fitted only on historical data to prevent lookahead bias. The current trading day's activity is passed through the fitted model.

5. Limitations & Risk Profile

Critical constraints of quantitative market microstructure analysis.

While tracking institutional flow on the IDX provides a significant informational advantage, the MyMantra system has distinct quantitative and structural limitations that must be acknowledged by researchers and operators.

Risk Vector Technical Detail
Survivorship Bias Only currently listed tickers are scored. Delisted stocks are excluded from historical back-tests, potentially skewing retrospective accuracy.
Look-Ahead in Dev Scoring weights (0.40, 0.30, etc.) were established observationally based on IDX market behavior; they lack strict optimization on a held-out test set.
Small Float Volatility While LOW-float stocks score higher due to the Float Amplifier interaction term, they intrinsically carry higher liquidity risk and susceptibility to rapid manipulation.
Z-Score Instability Brokers with fewer than 20 days of transaction history are excluded. New tickers or infrequently-traded brokers yield statistically unreliable standard deviations.
API Data Lag Index Alpha data is fetched end-of-day. Intra-day algorithmic execution by institutions is not captured until market close.
Single-Factor Risk The model is highly sensitive to broker behavior (40% weight). Sudden broker reclassifications invalidate historical baselines immediately.
